Sandbox Tab

A VPN inside one instance

Installing the hide.me client in an instance, and exactly whose traffic it covers.

The Sandbox tab can install the hide.me VPN client inside one instance. What it covers is the important part.

It covers that instance, and nothing else#

important

Not your Mac, and not your other instances

The tunnel is a WireGuard interface inside that one Linux. Your Mac's own traffic is untouched, and so is every other instance. A VPN switch in a Mac app looks like it covers the Mac, so this is worth being clear about.

That is also what makes it useful: one instance can be somewhere else while everything else on your Mac carries on normally.

What MSL does and does not do#

MSL doesMSL does not
Install the official client, at a pinned version whose exact contents it checksHandle your account, password or access token
Report whether the client is installed, whether the kernel can do WireGuard, whether you are logged in and whether a tunnel is upWrap the client's own commands in a button
Tell you the next step, in the client's own wordsConnect or disconnect for you

Connecting is you, in your own instance, with your own credentials:

The card tells you which of those is next.

The checks it reports#

CheckWhy it matters
InstalledThe client is present in the instance.
WireGuardThe instance's kernel can carry the tunnel. Without it the client cannot connect at all.
Logged inAn access token is present.
ConnectedA tunnel interface exists right now.

WireGuard is checked before you get as far as an account, because finding out afterwards is a poor way to spend ten minutes.

note

It needs the network gate open

A VPN is still network traffic. With the Sandbox's Network gate closed nothing connects, including this.
