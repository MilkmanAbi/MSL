Release tags: DNUO, UBP, FP and the rest

What the marker on a release tag means, and why a release can be mentioned without being offered.

A release tag can carry a marker saying who that release is for. It looks like this:

So v2.4.0-UBP-2.1 is version 2.4.0, marked UBP, with a minimum of 2.1.

The markers#

MarkerMeansIf you are below the minimum
(none)An ordinary release—
DNUODo not update older installsNever mentioned
UBPUnsafe-behaviour patch — offer it at onceMentioned, but you cannot take it
UBPDNUnsafe-behaviour patch, newer installs onlyNever mentioned
FPFeature patchMentioned, but you cannot take it
FBDNFeature patch, newer installs onlyNever mentioned

What the minimum is for#

The minimum is the oldest version of MSL a release is any use to.

A patch that fixes something introduced in 2.1 cannot help you if you are on 2.0 — the problem is not there. So v2.4.0-UBP-2.1 is urgent for anyone on 2.1 or newer, and for anyone older it is merely mentioned: you are told it exists, and told it is not for your version.

Mentioned, offered, or silent#

This is the difference the DN in a marker makes:

You are at or above the minimumYou are below it
UBP / FPOfferedMentioned, cannot be taken
UBPDN / FBDNOfferedNot mentioned at all
DNUOOfferedNot mentioned at all

A release being mentioned rather than offered is not a problem to solve. It means the newer version exists and does not apply to the copy you are running.

important

Urgent patches still reach you

A release marked UBP is one where the old behaviour can do something you would not want. Those are raised as soon as they are found, rather than waiting for the next time you happen to look — which is the whole reason the markers exist rather than every release being equal.
